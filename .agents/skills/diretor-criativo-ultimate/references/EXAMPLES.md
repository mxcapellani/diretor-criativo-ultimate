## Example 6 — Storyboard confirmation

### Initial user request

Create a cinematic automotive commercial.

### Expected first behavior

Return one cinematic prompt, PARAMS, and the storyboard question.

Do not generate the storyboard yet.

### Follow-up user request

Yes, create the storyboard.

### Expected storyboard structure

ACT 1: Arrival (Shots 1–3) — Establish the vehicle within its environment.

1. Extreme Wide Shot – Static – Introduce the illuminated road and distant vehicle.
2. Medium Long Shot – Dolly In – Reveal the front profile and design character.
3. Close-Up – Truck Right – Follow light reflections across the bodywork.

ACT 2: Motion (Shots 4–7) — Build movement and demonstrate physical performance.

4. Low Angle – Truck Right – Track the spinning wheels over reflective asphalt.
5. Full Shot – Orbit/Arc – Establish the vehicle's complete form.
6. Medium Shot – Pan Right – Follow the vehicle through a gradual turn.
7. Close-Up – Rack Focus – Shift attention from wet pavement to the front lights.

ACT 3: Hero Finish (Shots 8–10) — Conclude with a strong product composition.

8. Extreme Wide Shot – Crane/Jib/Boom – Reveal the full landscape around the road.
9. Medium Long Shot – Dolly In – Reestablish the vehicle as the dominant subject.
10. Low Angle – Static – Hold the final automotive hero composition.

In actual operation, the complete storyboard must remain consistent with the specific prompt already produced.

Do not change the vehicle's identity, environment, or campaign concept without instruction.

## Example 8 — Existing prompt refinement

### User request

Improve this prompt for Kling 3.0: "A runner in the rain, camera moving everywhere, epic lighting, cinematic."

### Expected behavior

Preserve the runner, rain, energy, and cinematic intention.

Replace ambiguous camera instructions with one controlled movement.

Describe visible lighting sources and physical interaction with rain.

Specify consistent direction of action.

Return the revised prompt in the standard format.

## Example 9 — Reference limitation

### User request

Use the attached image as a visual reference.

### Condition

The image is not accessible in the execution environment.

### Expected behavior

Do not describe unseen features.

Ask for a description only if no usable scene can be inferred.

If sufficient textual context exists, continue with a transparent assumption.

Never imply that the image was successfully analyzed.
